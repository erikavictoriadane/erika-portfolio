### Overview
When selling complex AI software to traditional, craft-oriented industries, product skepticism is often rooted in identity, not just feature sets. At Tastry, initial outbound campaigns were struggling with slow conversion cycles and defensive customer reactions. Winemakers viewed sensory AI as an existential threat—an automated machine claiming it could make better wine than a human artisan.

To diagnose the friction, I conducted in-depth qualitative customer discovery interviews with both our most vocal critics (such as the Director of Winemaking at Mendocino Wine Co.) and top enterprise prospects (including the Marketing Manager at J. Lohr). These conversations uncovered the psychological barriers holding back adoption. I used those insights to reposition Tastry from an automated decision-maker to an intuitive "GPS for wine creation," then rolled out targeted campaigns that accelerated lead conversion and closed marquee enterprise accounts.

**My Role:** Qualitative Customer Research · Prospect Interviews · Product Repositioning · Copywriting · Campaign Execution

### The Challenge
Winemaking is deeply rooted in personal expression and sensory craftsmanship. Early marketing positioned Tastry’s algorithms as an authoritative evaluation tool. The unintended consequence was immediate defensiveness:

- **Artisan Skepticism:** Winemakers feared the software would strip away their creative autonomy, telling them what wines to blend.
- **Abstract Value Proposition:** Marketing messaging highlighted generic AI sophistication rather than concrete commercial utility.
- **Misaligned Pain Points:** Outbound messaging failed to separate the needs of cellar production teams from the retail distribution priorities of winery marketing executives.

### The Strategy & Creative Approach
Customer discovery calls with key stakeholders revealed two critical breakthroughs:

> **The "GPS" Analogy (Mendocino Wine Co.):** Virginia explained that winemakers don't want a computer replacing their palate. I reframed Tastry using a GPS metaphor: *“Google Maps doesn't tell you where to go or take away the steering wheel; it helps you navigate around roadblocks and reach your destination faster.”* Positioning Tastry as a co-pilot for the artist eliminated threat and restored agency.

> **The "Competitive SKU" Formula (J. Lohr):** On a discovery call with Josh, Marketing Manager at J. Lohr, he shared the exact commercial trigger needed to convince leadership: *“If you wanted to sell tomorrow, all you have to do is show which SKU is beating their competitive set by X amount and which is losing, layered with your taste profiles.”*

### Key Takeaway / Learning
Prospect resistance is rarely about budget or technical specifications—it is almost always about perceived threat to professional identity. By listening closely to customer skepticism and reframing the technology as an empowering companion, marketing turned vocal critics into enterprise advocates.
