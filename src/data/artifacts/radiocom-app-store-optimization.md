### Overview
RADIO.COM was converting App Store impressions into installs at nearly three times the music-category benchmark. At first glance, that suggested our app store presence was performing well. But my competitive research revealed a different opportunity: we were missing out on discovery for some of the most relevant, high-volume search terms.

I researched how RADIO.COM compared with competing apps, examined keyword-level rankings and estimated installs, and developed recommendations for improving our app store positioning. The goal was to grow downloads and search visibility by understanding not just whether people converted when they saw us, but whether they were finding us in the first place.

*Brand context: This project dates to RADIO.COM’s original visual identity, before the company’s 2021 rebrand to Audacy. Screenshots and analysis reflect the app store landscape in July 2019.*

**My Role:** ASO Competitive Research · Search & Keyword Analysis · Conversion Benchmarking · App Listing Positioning · Stakeholder Presentation

### Finding the Competitive Gap
I looked beyond obvious brand competitors and focused on apps that ranked above RADIO.COM for the searches most relevant to our product. Simple Radio stood out. It ranked above us for “radio” on both iOS and Android, and the estimated iOS keyword-level install figures showed just how much discovery we were leaving to another app.

#### The Missed Opportunity in Generic Radio Searches
- For the keyword **“radio”**, the analysis estimated **3,585 monthly downloads** for Simple Radio compared with **381** for RADIO.COM.
- For **“radio app free”**, the figures were **3,726 estimated monthly downloads** for Simple Radio versus **278** for RADIO.COM.

Those numbers weren't total monthly app installs. They were keyword-attributed estimates in the research, useful for showing relative visibility and identifying which searches deserved attention.

#### Defending What Was Already Working
The analysis also revealed a strength worth protecting. **“Carplay”** and **“carplay apps”** were estimated to generate **829** and **936** monthly downloads for RADIO.COM, respectively, while competing apps each saw fewer than 20 downloads for those terms. Not every search was a problem; some were clear competitive advantages.

That mattered because the recommendation shouldn't simply be to chase every high-volume phrase. It needed to focus on the terms that fit the product, had meaningful demand, and represented a realistic opportunity to improve discovery.

### Turning Research Into Recommendations
I compared competitor keyword choices and listing copy with RADIO.COM's existing metadata, then developed a set of concrete proposed changes:

#### 01 / Make the Value Proposition Easier to Find
I recommended testing an app name that included a high-volume category keyword, with **“RADIO.COM – Music, News & Sports Radio!”** as an example. The idea was to describe the app's value clearly while aligning the listing with how consumers searched for radio.

#### 02 / Revisit the Subtitle and Keyword Fields
I proposed evaluating the subtitle for another relevant high-volume term, and reserving the keyword field for terms—including misspellings—better suited to metadata than to consumer-facing copy.

#### 03 / Make Listing Language More Deliberate
I also recommended rewriting the app description with clearer, more consistent references to target radio-related search phrases, informed by competitor keyword density. This was a recommendation in the 2019 deck, not evidence that repeating keywords in an app description would improve iOS search ranking; metadata fields and store algorithms need to be assessed by platform.

### From Research to Execution: Rewriting the App Store Description
I took the findings beyond the presentation and wrote a revised RADIO.COM app store description, turning the competitive keyword research into actual consumer-facing copy. The draft opened with **“RADIO.COM brings you unlimited free radio!”**—a clear statement of the product's value that also reflected the search language I'd identified.

I set explicit usage targets for terms such as “radio stations,” “free radio,” “free radio app,” “radio app,” and “radio!” and organized the description around real reasons to download: hundreds of radio stations, live sports coverage, music and news, podcasts, favorites, and listening on devices such as CarPlay and smart speakers.

Rather than presenting keywords in isolation, I used programming and feature examples to make the listing useful to a person deciding whether the app was right for them. The NFL coverage section, for example, paired a broad sports-listening benefit with specific teams and stations, while other sections highlighted local news, music discovery, and listening on the go.

*This completed copy draft demonstrates how I translated ASO analysis into an executable marketing asset. The materials do not independently confirm when or whether the description was published, or its effect on rankings and installs. Keyword repetition was part of my documented 2019 approach, not proof of ranking benefit on iOS.*

### Forecasting the Growth Opportunity
Using competitive keyword data and RADIO.COM's existing download performance, I developed an iOS acquisition forecast to quantify the potential impact of improving our visibility on high-value search terms.

By setting keyword-level download targets and comparing them against our existing performance, I identified an opportunity to generate approximately **5,948 additional monthly iOS downloads**—a projected **6% lift** over our 90-day baseline of 103,952 monthly downloads.

The forecast started with a 90-day baseline of 103,952 monthly iOS downloads. Meeting the keyword-level targets would bring the projected total to approximately 109,900 downloads per month—5,948 more than the baseline, or 5.7% growth (rounded to 6%).

Most of that potential was concentrated in four search terms:
- **“radio!”**: +2,800 projected monthly installs
- **“radio stations”**: +920 projected monthly installs
- **“radio app free”**: +725 projected monthly installs
- **“radio”**: +669 projected monthly installs

Together, those terms represented **5,114 of the 5,948 modeled incremental installs**. Breaking the forecast down by keyword made the acquisition opportunity measurable and helped prioritize where improved search visibility could matter most.

### Key Takeaways
This project reinforced that strong conversion performance doesn't necessarily mean a growth channel is fully optimized. Sometimes the bigger opportunity is one step earlier in the customer journey. By examining competitive search behavior alongside our own conversion metrics, I could identify where RADIO.COM was already strong—and where a clearer app store presence might help more listeners discover us.
