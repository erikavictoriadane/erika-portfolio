### Overview
Tastry pairs analytical chemistry with sensory AI algorithms to predict consumer flavor preferences and competitor benchmarks. While the platform held immense analytical power, most of that intelligence was locked inside the Tastry Insights Dashboard—a dense array of mass spectrometry charts, chemical variance matrices, and percentage distributions that non-technical winery executives found difficult to navigate.

When a client candidly confessed, *"I just feel stupid when I look at this dashboard,"* I knew our product communication had a usability problem. Winery founders and sales directors were trying to secure retail shelf space, but prospective grocery and wine buyers had neither the time nor the scientific background to decipher chemistry graphs. I bridged this gap by translating complex data into a concise, one-page "Retail Buyer Score Sheet" that sales teams and winery clients could take directly into commercial pitch meetings.

**My Role:** Customer Research · Information Architecture · Collateral Design · Sales Enablement · Product Automation

### The Challenge
Tastry’s existing collateral included a multi-page "technical sheet" showing granular compound chemistry and flavor breakdowns. For commercial decision-makers, it created friction rather than clarity:

- **Information Overload:** Retail buyers evaluate hundreds of wine submissions. A technical chemistry chart provides zero immediate guidance on whether a bottle will actually sell.
- **Missing Commercial Answers:** Buyers only care about three questions: How many consumers will like this wine? How does it stack up against category leaders? What market share will it capture?
- **Disjointed Workflow:** Outbound sales reps had to manually request custom consultant analyses every time they pitched an enterprise winery prospect.

![Original Tastry Insights Chemistry Dashboard — High Complexity for Non-Technical Buyers](/previews/tastry-score-cards-chemistry-dashboard.png)

### The Strategy & Creative Approach
To make the data commercially actionable, I worked backward from the buyer's purchasing decision:

> **Complex Lab Chemistry → Consumer Preference Synthesis → 1-Page Retail Scorecard**

Through interviews with enterprise marketing directors (including J. Lohr) and winery owners, I stripped away non-essential chemical indicators and focused the layout around a clear quadrant system:

- **Consumer Affinity Rating:** A single, prominent percentage showing predicted market appeal.
- **Benchmarked Competitive Set:** Head-to-head comparison against two top-performing category peers in the same price tier.
- **Sensory Quadrant Mapping:** Clean visual positioning showing flavor profile differentiation without scientific jargon.

Today, sales reps and clients can generate scoresheets on demand inside the portal, automatically pulling bottle photography, flavor quadrant plots, and dynamic bottle color accents matching the wine's hue.

### Key Takeaway / Learning
The most sophisticated AI or analytical technology is only as valuable as its ability to drive a commercial decision. When technology feels confusing, customers feel alienated. By translating complex data into the language of the buyer's business problem, marketing transformed an intimidating dashboard into an indispensable revenue driver.
