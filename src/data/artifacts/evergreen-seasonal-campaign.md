### Overview
What if the next successful campaign didn't require creating anything new? For Halloween 2025, I saw an opportunity to turn existing reputation-management resources into something timely, memorable, and useful to healthcare providers.

I developed two complementary Halloween-themed download offers, adapted the educational copy, and carried the creative concept across email, social imagery, landing pages, and downloadable guides.

**My Role:** Campaign Strategy · Creative Direction · Messaging & Copywriting · Content Repurposing · Integrated Marketing

### The Challenge
We already had useful educational content about collecting and responding to patient reviews. The challenge was finding a fresh way to bring those resources back to our audience—and turn everyday content into a reason to engage with WebMD Care.

### Finding the Seasonal Hook
The insight was simple: two common reputation-management tasks can feel genuinely intimidating. Asking patients for feedback takes confidence, and negative reviews can be frightening to receive.

Halloween gave me a natural creative angle for both.

> **Existing resources → Seasonal relevance → A fresh reason to engage**

Rather than dressing up an unrelated offer with Halloween graphics, I used the theme to make the value of each resource immediately recognizable.

### Giving Existing Content a New Life
I revisited existing review-request and review-response copy and wove Halloween language throughout the resources and promotional messaging. The underlying advice remained practical and useful long after the season ended; the creative packaging made it feel timely in October.

---

### Two Fears. Two Useful Offers.

#### 01 / How to Ask Your Patients for Feedback Without Fear
A downloadable guide featuring 12 fill-in-the-blank templates to help providers ask for patient reviews more confidently. The creative used a startled doctor, swirling smoke, and bats to make a familiar hesitation instantly relatable.

#### 02 / How to Respond to Frightening Patient Reviews
A second guide offered 13 customizable templates for responding to difficult patient feedback. A shocked doctor, low-star ratings, and a haunted visual environment made the problem—and the promise of help—clear at a glance.

### The Takeaway
A good campaign doesn't always start with a blank page. Sometimes the creative opportunity is seeing familiar content through a new lens—and finding the right moment to make it matter again.
